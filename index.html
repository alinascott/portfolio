Alina Scott, PhD

I am an Educational Technology Postdoctoral fellow in the College of Liberal Arts and History Department at the University of Texas. I support faculty in COLA interested in integrating innovative technologies and critical digital pedagogy into their courses.

My research explores the intersection of Black and Indigenous kinship within communities in the Eastern Woodlands of the United States in the nineteenth century. I apply historical analysis to digital humanities projects, archival storytelling, and public history initiatives.

Critical Archive & Digital Pedagogy

The Collector: Teaching With the F.A. Williams Collection

The University of Texas gained the collection of the sculptor Frederic Allen Williams. Once it reached Austin, it was split apart: his Diné (Navajo) textiles went to the Texas Memorial Museum, and later the Department of Art and Art History, and his photographs and lantern slides went to the Harry Ransom Center. The Collector brings the two archives back together, joining Woven into History (2018), a public history site on the textiles and their paper records, with Dr. Jesse Ritner's "Frederick Allen Williams: A Citizen Artist With A Magic Lantern" from the Ransom Center's lantern slides and ephemera.

It is built as a teaching tool for the critical archive. Students move through three pathways (Provenance, Textiles, and Photography) and trace how objects were collected, catalogued, divided, and described. Along the way they are asked two questions:

  • How do we tell stories when our sources are separated and silenced?
  • What do you know about Indigenous people, and how do you know it?

Open the full collection to explore the Provenance, Textiles, and Photography pathways, the teacher guide, and lesson plans.

Open The Collector

Source & Archival Provenance

Textiles and paper records from the Frederic Allen Williams Collection, University of Texas at Austin (Texas Memorial Museum accession, 1969). Lantern slides and ephemera from the Frederic Allen Williams Collection, Harry Ransom Center.

This collection is intended for educational use. It may contain harmful language and images of inappropriate dress, and should be read carefully and in the context of the teaching materials.
